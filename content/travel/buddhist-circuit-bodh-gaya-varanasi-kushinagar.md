India’s Buddhist heartland rewards patience. Monasteries, archaeological sites and living places of worship sit across long road sectors, so this plan uses eight days and avoids racing from monument to monument.

## Suggested itinerary

### Days 1–2 — Bodh Gaya

Arrive via Gaya or Patna and settle in before visiting the Mahabodhi Temple complex. Return early the next morning for quiet reflection, then explore monasteries established by Buddhist communities from different countries. Maintain silence where requested.

### Day 3 — Rajgir and Nalanda

Drive to Rajgir for Gridhakuta Hill and places associated with early Buddhist teaching. Continue to the archaeological remains of Nalanda. A trained interpreter adds essential historical context.

### Day 4 — Travel to Varanasi

Use this as a transfer and rest day. Long road journeys are tiring; schedule only a short orientation walk after arrival.

### Day 5 — Sarnath

Spend the day at Sarnath, associated with the Buddha’s first sermon. Visit the archaeological zone and museum subject to current opening rules, then allow time for contemplation.

### Days 6–7 — Kushinagar

Travel to Kushinagar, traditionally associated with the Buddha’s mahaparinirvana. Plan an unhurried site visit and a second morning for prayer or study before moving on.

### Day 8 — Departure

Depart from the most practical railhead or airport after checking current connections. A longer version can add Shravasti or Lumbini; the latter requires Nepal border and visa planning.

## Planning essentials

- Road sectors can be long; use a roadworthy vehicle with scheduled breaks.
- Check Archaeological Survey of India and museum closures near your dates.
- For cross-border Lumbini extensions, verify visas and entry rules separately.
- Groups should agree on respectful conduct at active monasteries.
