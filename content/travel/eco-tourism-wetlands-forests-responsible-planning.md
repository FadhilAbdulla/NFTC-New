Responsible eco-tourism begins with one landscape, not a string of distant wildlife sightings. This framework can be adapted to an appropriate wetland, forest buffer or community conservation destination after permits and carrying-capacity rules are checked.

## A six-day framework

### Day 1 — Gateway and briefing

Arrive at the regional gateway and meet the naturalist or local guide. Review wildlife distance, noise, waste and photography rules before entering the destination.

### Days 2–4 — One landscape in depth

Use dawn and late-afternoon windows for guided observation, leaving midday for rest, interpretation and community-led learning. Rotate smaller groups rather than crowding one trail or boat.

### Day 5 — Livelihood and conservation

Meet a cooperative, guide association, producer group or conservation initiative by agreement. Avoid token “village visits”; the host organisation should shape the encounter.

### Day 6 — Review and departure

Record sightings responsibly, share feedback and return without adding an unnecessary distant stop.

## Low-impact rules

- Use authorised guides and permitted routes only.
- Never feed, call, chase or crowd wildlife.
- Avoid geotagging sensitive nests or species locations.
- Carry out waste and minimise disposable plastic.
- Follow local restrictions even when another visitor does not.

## Official context and further reading

The Ministry of Tourism’s destination work includes eco-tourism and Amrit Dharohar-linked themes, while protected-area entry is controlled by the relevant authorities. Read the Ministry’s [schemes and guidelines](https://tourism.gov.in/schemes-and-guidelines/schemes) and use the chosen state or wildlife authority for current permits. This NFTCI framework becomes a proposed journey only after a specific destination is assessed.
