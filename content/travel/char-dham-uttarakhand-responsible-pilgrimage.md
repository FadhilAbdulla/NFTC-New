The Char Dham journey is physically demanding and highly seasonal. Altitude, mountain roads, weather and crowd conditions can change quickly. Twelve days is a planning minimum for many groups; health and safety must decide the final route.

## Suggested planning framework

### Days 1–3 — Gateway and Yamunotri sector

Begin from Haridwar, Rishikesh or Dehradun and move gradually into the hills. Keep a buffer before the Yamunotri approach. The walking route is not suitable for everyone; assess mobility support early and use only authorised services.

### Days 4–5 — Gangotri sector

Travel in daylight, pause for acclimatisation and keep the temple day otherwise light. Headache, nausea or unusual fatigue at altitude must be taken seriously.

### Days 6–9 — Kedarnath sector

Allow multiple days for access, weather disruption and recovery. Registration, transport controls and walking conditions should be rechecked immediately before movement. Do not promise a fixed timetable where mountain conditions control access.

### Days 10–11 — Badrinath sector

Continue only if the group is rested and conditions are suitable. Use one day for the main visit and one for contingency or nearby stops.

### Day 12 — Return

Descend with realistic road time and avoid a same-day flight connection after a long mountain transfer.

## Non-negotiable preparation

- Obtain appropriate medical advice before committing.
- Complete any official registration and carry identity documents.
- Build weather days into transport and accommodation arrangements.
- Confirm insurance scope, emergency contacts and cancellation terms.
- Avoid single-use plastic and follow local waste rules.

## Official context and further reading

Use only current Uttarakhand government registration, weather and route information when the yatra season opens. The Ministry of Tourism’s [schemes portal](https://tourism.gov.in/schemes-and-guidelines/schemes) explains destination-development support, but it is not a journey permit or booking channel.
