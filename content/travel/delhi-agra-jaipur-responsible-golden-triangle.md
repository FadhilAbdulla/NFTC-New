The Golden Triangle is popular for good reason, but it is often overpacked. This version limits the daily checklist and creates space for neighbourhoods, interpretation and local enterprise.

## Suggested itinerary

### Days 1–2 — Delhi

Use one day for Old Delhi and another for New Delhi rather than crossing the city repeatedly. Combine major heritage sites with a guided neighbourhood walk, and plan meals where groups can sit comfortably.

### Days 3–4 — Agra

Travel to Agra and keep the first afternoon light. Visit the Taj Mahal in a preselected time window subject to current ticketing and closure rules. Use the second day for Agra Fort and a responsible craft visit where purchasing is optional.

### Days 5–6 — Jaipur

Continue to Jaipur with a planned rest stop. Spread the palace and fort area across two sessions, allowing time for a small artisan workshop or textile cooperative. Depart only after checking realistic travel time to the airport or railway station.

## Better choices for groups

- Use licensed guides and official monument tickets.
- Avoid compulsory shopping stops or commission pressure.
- Ask for electric or lower-emission local transport where practical.
- Build heat and air-quality adjustments into the daily plan.
- Verify step-free access; historic sites vary widely.

## Official context and further reading

Consult the Ministry of Tourism’s [Incredible India portal](https://www.incredibleindia.gov.in/) and the relevant state tourism websites for current visitor information. Monument rules and ticketing may be controlled by the Archaeological Survey of India or another authority. This guide is a planning framework, not a government-certified package.
