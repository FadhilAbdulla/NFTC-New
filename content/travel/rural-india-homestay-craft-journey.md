This is not one fixed route. It is a method for building a responsible rural journey around two or three communities in the same region. The goal is meaningful hosting, fair local benefit and manageable expectations on both sides.

## A seven-day framework

### Day 1 — Regional gateway

Meet the local coordinator, discuss etiquette and review what has been agreed with host communities. Avoid arriving in a village unannounced.

### Days 2–3 — First community

Stay in a verified homestay or nearby accommodation. Join activities that hosts have chosen to share: a farm walk, cooking session, craft demonstration or local history. Participation should never disrupt livelihoods.

### Day 4 — Slow transfer

Travel to the second cluster with a market, landscape or cooperative stop on the way. Keep purchases voluntary and explain how prices are set.

### Days 5–6 — Second community

Explore another livelihood or cultural landscape. Build in a facilitated conversation about local priorities, especially for educational groups.

### Day 7 — Reflection and return

Close with feedback for hosts and travellers, then return to the gateway.

## Principles for a fair visit

- Agree group size, activities, photography and payments beforehand.
- Do not turn private homes or ceremonies into open attractions.
- Pay hosts and facilitators transparently.
- Use local food and transport where safe and practical.
- Leave room for hosts to say no or change the plan.

## Official context and further reading

The Ministry of Tourism has published strategies for [rural tourism and rural homestays](https://tourism.gov.in/tourism-programmes/rural-tourism). Swadesh Darshan 2.0 also emphasises sustainable destination development and local livelihoods. These policies guide good practice but do not certify every homestay. NFTCI can explore a region-specific concept with suitable cooperative and community partners.
